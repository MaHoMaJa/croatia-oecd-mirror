01 — Context

What the OECD is, and why
it matters for Croatia

The Organisation for Economic Co-operation and Development is among the most influential international bodies shaping public policy. Its statistical, analytical and normative output reaches education, health, economic and research policy in member countries and in candidates — Croatia among them.

1961
Founded

Successor to the OEEC, set up in 1948 to co-ordinate Marshall Plan reconstruction in Europe.[1]

38
Member countries

Developed democracies with market economies; official languages English and French.[1]

1,250+
Titles a year

Alongside more than 500 major reports and country surveys and over 5 billion data points annually.[2][3]

Croatia and the OECD: a timeline

Accession discussions were opened on 25 January 2022 by decision of the OECD Council, and the accession Roadmap (C/MIN(2022)23/FINAL) was adopted at the Ministerial Council on 10 June 2022.[21][22]

The process runs through in-depth reviews in up to 25 technical committees, which assess how far legislation, policy and practice align with OECD standards — from education and health to the judiciary, corporate governance and innovation policy.[22]

03 — The production route

A publication that has
to be approved

This is a distinction worth explaining to any user who cites an OECD report as though it were a peer-reviewed article. The Council, more than 300 committees and the Secretariat together determine what comes out: analysis is planned, initiated, reviewed and eventually declassified.[4]

01

Drafting in the Secretariat

The responsible directorate writes the draft under the programme of work approved by the Council. The Secretariat numbers some 3,300 staff across directorates reporting to the Secretary-General.[4]

02

Discussion in committee

Committees cover the same fields as government ministries and take decisions by consensus. This is precisely the stage at which member states can shape the wording.[4]

03

Declassification

The committee formally clears the text for the public. Example: the 2023 OECD Digital Government Index was declassified by the Public Governance Committee on 19 January 2024.[6] Released material keeps its original reference, such as TAD/TC/WP(2015)18/FINAL, which is what lets you retrieve the source document in the official documents system.[7][8]

04

Release, with a caveat

Unless otherwise stated, material is issued under the responsibility of the Secretary-General and does not necessarily reflect the official views of member countries.[5] Working papers add that they present preliminary results and are published to stimulate discussion.

When the review is the book

The Economic and Development Review Committee sits at the core of the peer review mechanism. Each economy is examined about every two years: the Committee designates two lead examiners, a Secretariat draft serves as the basis for discussion, the text is revised in consultation with the country under review after the plenary session, and the Committee then approves it by consensus.[9]

The Economic Surveys of Croatia of 2023 and 2026 came through exactly this route. Worth keeping in mind when such a report is cited as an independent external assessment.

04 — Licensing

One date splits the
collection in two

The question a library actually gets is not "where is that report" but "may I put this table in my paper, translate it, republish it". The answer does not depend on the title. It depends on the date of publication.

Open access regime

Creative Commons CC BY 4.0, by default

  • ✓Copy and redistribute for any purpose, including commercial
  • ✓Adapt and translate without seeking authorisation
  • ✓Covers publications, working papers, journal articles, policy papers, briefs, case studies and country notes
  • !Attribution required; translations must note that the original prevails in case of discrepancy
  • !Adaptations must state that the views are not those of the OECD or its member countries
  • ✕Does not cover third-party material inside the publication
  • ✕CC BY-NC 4.0 or CC BY-ND 4.0 may apply exceptionally — check the notice in the work itself
Disputes: arbitration under Permanent Court of Arbitration rules, seat in Paris[12][10]
Legacy regime

Permission on request, with exceptions

  • ✕Commercial translation requires authorisation (comrights@oecd.org)
  • ✓Where granted, authorisation is free of charge and non-exclusive
  • !Some older material was already under Creative Commons licences — check the copyright notice
  • !PISA products — reports, questionnaires, questions and sample tasks — were under CC BY-NC-SA 3.0 IGO
  • ✕IEA material has its own regime: logos and visual identity require written permission
Data may be owned by third parties; the OECD does not warrant that it holds all rights[11][13][14]
Rule of thumb for the desk

Check the year of publication first, then the copyright page inside the PDF. The regime is set by the date of publication, not the date of download: material first published before 2024 and reposted since keeps the regime of its original release.[11]

08 — Interpretation

Gaps and openings:
what the data tells us

The analysis reveals not only where Croatian scholarship is present in OECD documents, but where it is systematically absent — and what that means for the research community and for policy actors.

Gap

Thin presence in economic analysis

Despite a long tradition of economic research, Croatian work is rarely cited in Economic Surveys and country profiles.

Gap

Output not published in English

A substantial body of valuable research stays out of reach of OECD analysts because it is neither published nor translated into English.

Gap

Low visibility in STI documents

Croatia is weakly represented in the Science, Technology and Innovation Outlook — a key document for positioning research policy.

Opening

Growth in education policy

Citation of Croatian research is rising in OECD education documents, particularly around PISA analysis and curricular reform.

Opening

Accession as a catalyst

Accession reviews necessarily analyse Croatian policy — a structural opening for domestic expertise to become more visible.

Opening

A regional position for the institute

IRMO can position itself as a regional expert on South East Europe in the OECD context, filling a gap in regional analysis.

Recommendations for the research community

Drawn from the visibility count and the gaps identified above.

01
Track Overton systematicallyIntroduce regular monitoring of Croatian institutions in the policy database as part of research impact evaluation.
02
A strategy for publishing in EnglishPrioritise international publication in fields where domestic expertise is strong and visibility is weak.
03
Engage the directoratesContact the relevant OECD directorates directly and take part in public consultations.
04
Publish open accessOpenly available work is markedly more visible in policy databases. Use DABAR and CROSBI consistently.
